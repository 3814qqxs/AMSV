## E. Full proposed chapter text (Matthew 15)

## Matthew 15

---
1  Then there come to Yeshua from Jerusalem Pharisees and scribes, saying,
2  “Why do your disciples transgress the tradition of the elders? for they wash not their hands when they eat bread.”[br]
» wash not their hands when they eat bread :: *netilat yadayim* — ritual hand-washing before bread is a rabbinic ordinance (*m. Yadayim*; *b. Sotah* 4b), not a Torah command; Exodus 30:19–21 requires it only of priests at service. "The tradition of the elders" is the oral-law (halakhic) chain.
3  And He answered and said to them, “Why do you all also transgress the commandment of God because of your tradition?[br]
4  For God said, ‘Honor your father and your mother’: and, ‘Whoever speaks evil of father or mother, let him surely die.’
→ Honor your father and your mother :: Exodus 20:12; Deuteronomy 5:16
→ Whoever speaks evil of father or mother, let him surely die :: Exodus 21:17; Leviticus 20:9; Proverbs 20:20
» Whoever speaks evil of father or mother :: *meqallel* (Exodus 21:17, "curses, treats as light") — the root is the opposite of *kaved* ("honor," lit. "treat as weighty") in Exodus 20:12. The LXX renders it *kakologōn*, the same Greek word Matthew uses.
5  But you all say, Whoever shall say to his father or his mother, ‘Whatever you might have gained from me is a gift’;[br]
→ is a gift :: Numbers 30:2; Leviticus 1:2
» Whatever you might have gained from me is a gift :: The functional equivalent of a *qorban* vow (cf. Mark 7:11, which names it) — a formal dedication of an asset to the Temple placing it permanently beyond ordinary use; the Mishnah devotes a whole tractate (*Nedarim*) to such vows and the narrow grounds for release from them.
6  he shall not honor his father. And you all have made void the word of God because of your tradition.[br]
7  You all hypocrites, well did Isaiah prophesy of you, saying,
8  ‘This people honors Me with their lips;
      But their heart is far from Me.
→ This people honors Me with their lips :: Isaiah 29:13
9  But in vain do they worship Me,
      Teaching as their doctrines the precepts of men.’”[br]
» in vain do they worship Me :: Matthew follows the LXX (*matēn de sebontai me, didaskontes entalmata anthrōpōn*); the Hebrew (MT) reads "their fear of Me is a commandment of men, learned by rote" (*mitzvat anashim melumadah*). The quotation is Septuagintal, not a different text.
10  And He called to Him the multitude, and said to them, “Hear, and understand:[br]
11  Not that which enters into the mouth defiles the man; but that which proceeds out of the mouth, this defiles the man.”[br]
12  Then came the disciples, and said to Him, “Do you know that the Pharisees were offended, when they heard this saying?”[br]
13  But He answered and said, “Every plant which My heavenly Father did not plant, shall be rooted up.[br]
→ Every plant which My heavenly Father did not plant, shall be rooted up :: Isaiah 60:21; Isaiah 61:3; Jeremiah 45:4
14  Let them alone: they are blind guides. And if the blind guide the blind, both shall fall into a pit.”[br]
→ they are blind guides :: Isaiah 56:10; Isaiah 9:16
15  And Peter answered and said to Him, “Explain to us the parable.”[br]
16  And He said, “Are you all also still without understanding?[br]
17  Do you all not perceive that whatever goes into the mouth passes into the belly, and is cast out into the latrine?[br]
18  But the things which proceed out of the mouth come forth out of the heart; and they defile the man.[br]
19  For out of the heart come forth evil thoughts,[br] murders, adulteries, fornications, thefts,[br] false witness, slanders:[br]
→ evil thoughts :: Genesis 6:5; Genesis 8:21
→ murders :: Exodus 20:13–16
20  these are the things which defile the man; but to eat with unwashed hands defiles not the man.”
» unwashed hands :: The question throughout (vv.2, 20) is the rabbinic *netilat yadayim*; Yeshua's conclusion addresses that, not the Torah's food distinctions (Leviticus 11). *koinoi* ("makes common") is the verb behind "defiles" (vv.11, 18, 20).
---
21  And Yeshua went out from there, and withdrew into the district of Tyre and Sidon.
→ Tyre and Sidon :: 1 Kings 17:9–24
22  And behold, a Canaanite woman came out from that region, and cried, saying, “Have mercy on me, O Lord, You son of David; my daughter is severely tormented by a demon.”[br]
→ a Canaanite woman :: Joshua 2:9–11; Joshua 6:25
23  But He answered her not a word. And His disciples came and begged Him, saying, “Send her away; for she cries after us.”[br]
24  But He answered and said, “I was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.”[br]
→ the lost sheep of the house of Israel :: Jeremiah 50:6; Ezekiel 34:16
25  But she came and worshipped Him, saying, “Lord, help me.”[br]
26  And He answered and said, “It is not right to take the children’s bread and cast it to the dogs.”[br]
27  But she said, “Yes, Lord: for even the dogs eat of the crumbs which fall from their masters’ table.”[br]
» for even the dogs eat of the crumbs which fall from their masters’ table :: Turns Yeshua's own figure back on itself without contesting the priority it states (Israel first) — a *qal va-chomer* in miniature, arguing from the lesser portion, crumbs, to a legitimate claim on it.
28  Then Yeshua answered and said to her, “O woman, great is your faith: let it be done for you as you wish.” And her daughter was healed from that hour.
---
29  And Yeshua departed from there, and came near to the sea of Galilee; and He went up into the mountain, and sat there.
30  And there came to Him great multitudes, having with them the lame, blind, mute, maimed, and many others, and they cast them down at His feet; and He healed them:[br]
31  so that the multitude marveled, when they saw the mute speaking, the maimed whole, and the lame walking, and the blind seeing: and they glorified the God of Israel.
→ the mute speaking, the maimed whole, and the lame walking, and the blind seeing :: Isaiah 35:5–6
32  And Yeshua called to Him His disciples, and said, “I have compassion on the multitude, because they have stayed with Me for three days now and have nothing to eat: and I do not want to send them away hungry, lest perhaps they faint on the way.”[br]
33  And the disciples say to Him, “From where could we get so many loaves in a desolate place as to fill so great a multitude?”[br]
34  And Yeshua said to them, “How many loaves have you all?” And they said, “Seven, and a few small fish.”[br]
35  And He commanded the multitude to sit down on the ground;[br]
36  and He took the seven loaves and the fish; and He gave thanks and broke, and gave to the disciples, and the disciples to the multitudes.[br]
37  And they all ate, and were filled: and they took up what was left over of the broken pieces, seven baskets full.[br]
» seven baskets full :: *spyridas* (large provision baskets; cf. Acts 9:25) — distinct from *kophinous* at 14:20; Matthew 16:9–10 keeps the two terms apart.
38  And those who ate were four thousand men, besides women and children.
39  And He sent away the multitudes, and entered into the boat, and came into the region of Magadan.
