# James

## James 1

1  Jacob, a servant of God and of the Lord Yeshua Messiah, to the twelve tribes which are of the Dispersion, greeting.
» Jacob :: *Iakōbos* (Ἰάκωβος), "Jacob" — the same name as the patriarch. English "James" descends from a medieval phonetic drift (Iacobus > Iacomus > James); the book title keeps that traditional English form, but the author names himself Jacob in the text. Traditionally identified as Yeshua's half-brother (Galatians 1:19) and leader of the Jerusalem assembly (Acts 15).
>>>
2  Count it all joy, my brothers, [br] when you all fall into trials of many kinds;
» trials of many kinds :: *peirasmois poikilois* (πειρασμοῖς ποικίλοις) — *peirasmos* covers both "trial/testing" (external hardship) and "temptation" (internal enticement to sin). God permits trials — adverse circumstances that call for a choice of obedient faith despite them (cf. Cain, Genesis 4:6-7; Peter's sifting, Luke 22:31-32) — but is never Himself the source of the pull toward sin (v13). Jacob runs the same word across this whole opening, using it first for testing (vv2-12) before pivoting explicitly to the sin-sense at v13.
3  knowing that the proving of your faith works patience.
4  And let patience have its perfect work, [br] that you all may be perfect and entire, [br] lacking in nothing. [br]
5  But if any of you lacks wisdom, [br] let him ask of God, [br] who gives to all liberally and is without reproach; [br] and it shall be given him.
6  But let him ask in faith, without doubting: [br] for he that doubts is like the surge of the sea driven by the wind and tossed. [br]
7  For let not that man think that he shall receive anything of the Lord; [br]
8  a doubleminded man, unstable in all his ways. [br]
» doubleminded :: *dipsychos* (δίψυχος), lit. "two-souled" — a word apparently coined by Jacob (unattested earlier in Greek); it recurs at 4:8, where he calls the doubleminded to purify their hearts.
9  But let the brother of low degree glory in his high estate:
10  and the rich, in that he is made low: [br] because as the flower of the grass he shall pass away.
11  For the sun arises with the scorching wind, and withers the grass; [br] and the flower thereof falls, and the beauty of its appearance perishes: [br] so also shall the rich man fade away in his goings.
---
12  Blessed is the man that endures trial; [br] for when he has been approved, [br] he shall receive the crown of life, [br] which the Lord promised to them that love Him.
» endures trial :: same word family as v2 (*peirasmon*) — still the "trial/testing" sense; the pivot to the sin-sense comes at v13.
>>>
13  Let no man say when he is tempted, “I am tempted of God”; [br] for God cannot be tempted with evil, [br] and He Himself tempts no man:
14  but each man is tempted, [br] when he is drawn away by his own lust, and enticed.
» drawn away by his own lust, and enticed :: *exelkomenos kai deleazomenos* (ἐξελκόμενος καὶ δελεαζόμενος) — hunting and fishing language, "dragged out [of cover]" and "baited [with a lure]." Desire stalks and lures a person the way a hunter stalks game.
15  Then the lust, when it has conceived, bears sin: [br] and the sin, when it is fullgrown, brings forth death.
>>>
16  Be not deceived, my beloved brothers. [br]
17  Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, [br] coming down from the Father of lights, [br] with whom can be no variation, neither shadow that is cast by turning.
» Father of lights :: ties God's changelessness to His own creation of the heavenly lights (Genesis 1:14-18) — the sun, moon, and stars vary and cast shadows as they move; their Maker does not.
18  Of His own will He brought us forth by the word of truth, [br] that we should be a kind of firstfruits of His creatures.
→ a kind of firstfruits :: Leviticus 23:10
» brought us forth :: *apekyēsen* ("brought forth/gave birth to") is the same verb translated "brings forth" at v15 — Jacob sets God's birth-by-truth deliberately against sin's birth-by-lust; "firstfruits" (*aparchē*) evokes the Torah's firstfruits offering, the first and best portion consecrated to God.
>>>
19  You all know this, my beloved brothers. [br] But let every man be swift to hear, [br] slow to speak, [br] slow to wrath:
20  for the wrath of man works not the righteousness of God.
21  Therefore putting away all filthiness and overflowing of wickedness, [br] receive with meekness the implanted word, [br] which is able to save your souls.
→ the implanted word :: Mark 4:14-20
22  But be you all doers of the word, [br] and not hearers only, [br] deluding your own selves. [br]
23  For if any one is a hearer of the word and not a doer, [br] he is like to a man beholding his natural face in a mirror:
24  for he beholds himself, [br] and goes away, [br] and immediately forgets what manner of man he was.
25  But he that looks into the perfect Torah, the Torah of liberty, [br] and so continues, [br] being not a hearer that forgets but a doer that works, [br] this man shall be blessed in his doing.
» the perfect Torah, the Torah of liberty :: Jacob reads Torah through Yeshua's own re-teaching of it (cf. 2:8's "royal law" = Leviticus 19:18) — its ethical core, which he calls liberating rather than restrictive.
>>>
26  If any man thinks himself to be religious, [br] while he bridles not his tongue but deceives his heart, [br] this man’s religion is vain.
» bridles not his tongue :: the same bridle-image Jacob develops at length in 3:2-3 (a bit in a horse's mouth); the tongue theme announced here gets a full chapter later.
27  Pure religion and undefiled before our God and Father is this, [br] to visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, [br] and to keep oneself unspotted from the world.
→ to visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction :: Deuteronomy 10:18

## James 2

1  My brothers, hold not the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory, with respect of persons.
2  For if there come into your synagogue a man with a gold ring, in fine clothing, and there come in also a poor man in vile clothing;
3  and you all have regard to him that wears the fine clothing, and say, Sit you here in a good place; and you all say to the poor man, Stand you there, or sit under my footstool;
4  do you all not make distinctions among yourselves, and become judges with evil thoughts?
5  Hearken, my beloved brothers; did not God choose them that are poor as to the world to be rich in faith, and heirs of the kingdom which he promised to them that love him?
6  But you all have dishonored the poor man. Do not the rich oppress you, and themselves drag you before the judgment-seats?
7  Do not they blaspheme the honorable name by which you all are called?
8  However if you all fulfil the royal law, according to the scripture, You shall love your neighbor as thyself, you all do well:
9  but if you all have respect of persons, you all commit sin, being convicted by the law as transgressors.
10  For whoever shall keep the whole law, and yet stumble in one point, he is become guilty of all.
11  For he that said, Do not commit adultery, said also, Do not kill. Now if you do not commit adultery, but kill, you are become a transgressor of the law.
12  So speak you all, and so do, as men that are to be judged by a law of liberty.
13  For judgment is without mercy to him that has showed no mercy: mercy glories against judgment.
14  What does it profit, my brothers, if a man say he has faith, but have not works? can that faith save him?
15  If a brother or sister be naked and in lack of daily food,
16  and one of you say to them, Go in peace, be you all warmed and filled; and yet you all give them not the things needful to the body; what does it profit?
17  Even so faith, if it have not works, is dead in itself.
18  Indeed, a man will say, You have faith, and I have works: show me your faith apart from your works, and I by my works will show you my faith.
19  You believe that God is one; you doe well: the demons also believe, and shudder.
20  But will you know, O vain man, that faith apart from works is barren?
21  Was not Abraham our father justified by works, in that he offered up Isaac his son upon the altar?
22  You see that faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect;
23  and the scripture was fulfilled which says, And Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him for righteousness; and he was called the friend of God.
24  You all see that by works a man is justified, and not only by faith.
25  And in like manner was not also Rahab the harlot justified by works, in that she received the messengers, and sent them out another way?
26  For as the body apart from the spirit is dead, even so faith apart from works is dead.

## James 3

1  Be not many of you teachers, my brothers, knowing that we shall receive heavier judgment.
2  For in many things we all stumble. If any stumbles not in word, the same is a perfect man, able to bridle the whole body also.
3  Now if we put the horses’ bridles into their mouths that they may obey us, we turn about their whole body also.
4  Behold, the ships also, though they are so great and are driven by rough winds, are yet turned about by a very small rudder, where the impulse of the steersman wills.
5  So the tongue also is a little member, and boasts great things. Behold, how much wood is kindled by how small a fire!
6  And the tongue is a fire: the world of iniquity among our members is the tongue, which defiles the whole body, and sets on fire the wheel of nature, and is set on fire by hell.
7  For every kind of beasts and birds, of creeping things and things in the sea, is tamed, and has been tamed by mankind:
8  but the tongue can no man tame; it is a restless evil, it is full of deadly poison.
9  Therewith bless we the Lord and Father; and therewith curse we men, who are made after the likeness of God:
10  out of the same mouth comes forth blessing and cursing. My brothers, these things ought not so to be.
11  Does the fountain send forth from the same opening sweet water and bitter?
12  Can a fig tree, my brothers, yield olives, or a vine figs? neither can salt water yield sweet.
13  Who is wise and understanding among you? let him show by his good life his works in meekness of wisdom.
14  But if you all have bitter jealousy and faction in your heart, glory not and lie not against the truth.
15  This wisdom is not a wisdom that comes down from above, but is earthly, sensual, devilish.
16  For where jealousy and faction are, there is confusion and every vile deed.
17  But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, easy to be entreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without variance, without hypocrisy.
18  And the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace for them that make peace.

## James 4

1  From where come wars and from where come fightings among you? come they not hence, even of your pleasures that war in your members?
2  You all lust, and have not: you all kill, and covet, and cannot obtain: you all fight and war; you all have not, because you all ask not.
3  You all ask, and receive not, because you all ask amiss, that you all may spend it in your pleasures.
4  You all adulteresses, know you all not that the friendship of the world is enmity with God? Whoever therefore would be a friend of the world makes himself an enemy of God.
5  Or think you all that the scripture speaks in vain? Does the spirit which he made to dwell in us long to envying?
6  But he gives more grace. Therefore the scripture says, God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble.
7  Be subject therefore to God; but resist the devil, and he will flee from you.
8  Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you. Cleanse your hands, you all sinners; and purify your hearts, you all doubleminded.
9  Be afflicted, and mourn, and weep: let your laughter be turned to mourning, and your joy to heaviness.
10  Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he shall exalt you.
11  Speak not one against another, brothers. He that speaks against a brother, or judges his brother, speaks against the law, and judges the law: but if you judge the law, you are not a doer of the law, but a judge.
12  One only is the lawgiver and judge, even he who is able to save and to destroy: but who are you that judge your neighbor?
13  Come now, you all that say, To-day or to-morrow we will go into this city, and spend a year there, and trade, and get gain:
14  whereas you all know not what shall be the next day. What is your life? For you all are a vapor that appears for a little time, and then vanishes away.
15  For that you all ought to say, If the Lord will, we shall both live, and do this or that.
16  But now you all glory in your vauntings: all such glorying is evil.
17  To him therefore that knows to do good, and does it not, to him it is sin.

## James 5

1  Come now, you all rich, weep and howl for your miseries that are coming upon you.
2  Your riches are corrupted, and your garments are moth-eaten.
3  Your gold and your silver are rusted; and their rust shall be for a testimony against you, and shall eat your flesh as fire. You all have laid up your treasure in the last days.
4  Behold, the hire of the laborers who mowed your fields, which is of you kept back by fraud, cries out: and the cries of them that reaped have entered into the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth.
5  You all have lived delicately on the earth, and taken your pleasure; you all have nourished your hearts in a day of slaughter.
6  You all have condemned, you all have killed the righteous one; he does not resist you.
7  Be patient therefore, brothers, until the coming of the Lord. Behold, the husbandman waits for the precious fruit of the earth, being patient over it, until it receive the early and latter rain.
8  Be you all also patient; establish your hearts: for the coming of the Lord is at hand.
9  Murmur not, brothers, one against another, that you all be not judged: behold, the judge stands before the doors.
10  Take, brothers, for an example of suffering and of patience, the prophets who spoke in the name of the Lord.
11  Behold, we call them blessed that endured: you all have heard of the patience of Job, and have seen the end of the Lord, how that the Lord is full of pity, and merciful.
12  But above all things, my brothers, swear not, neither by the heaven, nor by the earth, nor by any other oath: but let your yes be yes, and your no, no; that you all fall not under judgment.
13  Is any among you suffering? let him pray. Is any cheerful? let him sing praise.
14  Is any among you sick? let him call for the elders of the church; and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord:
15  and the prayer of faith shall save him that is sick, and the Lord shall raise him up; and if he have committed sins, it shall be forgiven him.
16  Confess therefore your sins one to another, and pray one for another, that you all may be healed. The supplication of a righteous man avails much in its working.
17  Elijah was a man of like passions with us, and he prayed fervently that it might not rain; and it rained not on the earth for three years and six months.
18  And he prayed again; and the heaven gave rain, and the earth brought forth her fruit.
19  My brothers, if any among you err from the truth, and one convert him;
20  let him know, that he who converts a sinner from the error of his way shall save a soul from death, and shall cover a multitude of sins.
