# Jude

## Jude 1

1  Judah, a servant of Yeshua Messiah, and brother of Jacob, to them that are called, beloved in God the Father, and kept for Yeshua Messiah:
» Judah :: *Ioudas* — the same name as "Judah"; "Jude" was a traditional English convention distinguishing this author from Judas Iscariot.
2  Mercy to you and peace and love be multiplied.
>>>
3  Beloved, while I was giving all diligence to write to you of our common salvation, I was constrained to write to you exhorting you to contend earnestly for the faith which was once for all delivered to the saints.
» once for all delivered :: *hapax paradotheisē* — a fixed, closed body of apostolic teaching already handed down, not something still being formed; the language of "delivering" and "receiving" a tradition intact echoes the rabbinic transmission-chain idiom later codified in the Mishnah's *Pirkei Avot* ("Moses received the Torah from Sinai and delivered it..."), applied here to the apostolic gospel.
4  For there are certain men crept in secretly, even they who were of old written of beforehand to this condemnation, ungodly men, turning the grace of our God into lasciviousness, and denying our only Master and Lord, Yeshua Messiah.
» written of beforehand :: points forward to the Enoch prophecy Judah cites directly at v14-15 — these teachers' judgment was not a late improvisation but foretold.
---
5  Now I desire to put you in remembrance, though you all know all things once for all, that Yeshua, having saved a people out of the land of Egypt, afterward destroyed them that believed not.
» Yeshua, having saved a people out of the land of Egypt :: the earliest and best witnesses (P72, Sinaiticus, Vaticanus, Alexandrinus) read "Yeshua" rather than ASV's "the Lord" here — Yeshua as the one who led Israel out of Egypt and later judged the unbelieving generation, an explicit pre-existence Christology.
6  And angels that kept not their own principality, but left their proper habitation, He has kept in everlasting bonds under darkness to the judgment of the great day.
→ angels that kept not their own principality :: Genesis 6:1-4
» angels that kept not their own principality :: the base narrative is Genesis 6:1-4's "sons of God," read through the Second-Temple lens of *1 Enoch*'s Book of the Watchers (chs. 6-16), which develops the same fallen-angel judgment tradition in far more detail; 2 Peter 2:4 draws on the identical tradition.
7  Even as Sodom and Gomorrah, and the cities about them, having in like manner with these given themselves over to fornication and gone after strange flesh, are set forth as an example, suffering the punishment of eternal fire.
→ Sodom and Gomorrah :: Genesis 19:1-25
» strange flesh :: *sarkos heteras* ("other/different flesh") — read by some as pursuit of the visiting angels' non-human nature (Genesis 19:1-5), by others as characterizing the vice itself; the Greek allows either.
---
8  Yet in like manner these also in their dreamings defile the flesh, and reject authority, and rail at the glorious ones.
» reject authority :: *athetousin* ("set aside, repudiate") — a different Greek word from Mark 9:12's "set at nought" (*exoudeneō*), despite ASV using the same English phrase there; also distinct from 1 Corinthians 1:19, 1:28's "thwart"/"nullify" (*atheteō* with a different object).
» the glorious ones :: *doxas*, literally "glories" — the angelic powers, not human office-holders; "dignities" (ASV) risked being misread as civic rank.
9  But Michael the archangel, when contending with the devil he disputed about the body of Moses, dared not bring against him a railing judgment, but said, “The Lord rebuke you.”
→ The Lord rebuke you :: Zechariah 3:2
» disputed about the body of Moses :: not in the Hebrew Bible — early church writers (Origen, Clement of Alexandria) trace this episode to a lost Second-Temple work, the *Assumption of Moses*; Judah treats it as known, reliable tradition without needing to argue for its source.
10  But these rail at whatever things they know not: and what they understand naturally, like the creatures without reason, in these things are they destroyed.
---
11  Woe to them! for they went in the way of Cain, and rushed headlong into the error of Balaam for hire, and perished in the gainsaying of Korah.
→ the way of Cain :: Genesis 4:3-8
→ the error of Balaam :: Numbers 22:1-24:25
→ the gainsaying of Korah :: Numbers 16:1-35
» rushed headlong :: *exechythēsan* ("poured themselves out, abandoned themselves").
---
12  These are they who are hidden rocks in your love-feasts when they feast with you, shepherds that without fear feed themselves; [br] clouds without water, carried along by winds; [br] autumn trees without fruit, twice dead, plucked up by the roots; [br]
» hidden rocks :: *spilades* — could equally mean "stains/blemishes" (cf. *spiloi*, the same image at 2 Peter 2:13); the Greek supports either "reef" or "blot," and the ASV's "hidden rocks" takes the former.
» love-feasts :: *agapais* — the communal fellowship meal of the earliest assemblies, held alongside (and eventually distinguished from) the Lord's Supper; cf. 1 Corinthians 11:20-22.
13  wild waves of the sea, foaming out their own shame; [br] wandering stars, for whom the blackness of darkness has been reserved for ever.
» wandering stars :: *planētai asteres* — ancient astronomy's term for the non-fixed lights (the origin of "planet"), useless for the navigation and calendar-reckoning fixed stars provided; the same image organizes much of *1 Enoch*'s astronomical material, which associates wandering stars with the fallen Watchers of v6 — setting up the direct Enoch citation at v14-15.
---
14  And to these also Enoch, the seventh from Adam, prophesied, saying, “Behold, the Lord came with ten thousands of His holy ones, [br]
→ Enoch, the seventh from Adam :: Genesis 5:18-24
» Enoch, the seventh from Adam, prophesied :: a direct quotation of *1 Enoch* 1:9, a Second-Temple Jewish apocalyptic work outside the Hebrew canon — Jude cites it as genuine prophecy, one of the NT's clearest uses of a non-canonical Jewish text as authoritative.
15  to execute judgment upon all, and to convict all the ungodly of all their works of ungodliness which they have ungodly wrought, and of all the hard things which ungodly sinners have spoken against Him.”
---
16  These are murmurers, complainers, walking after their lusts (and their mouth speaks great swelling words), showing respect of persons for the sake of advantage.
» murmurers :: *gongystai* — the same word-family the LXX uses for Israel's wilderness grumbling (e.g. Numbers 14:27); a recurring Torah type for faithless complaint against God's provision, applied here to the false teachers.
>>>
17  But you all, beloved, remember you all the words which have been spoken before by the apostles of our Lord Yeshua Messiah;
18  that they said to you, “In the last time there shall be mockers, walking after their own ungodly lusts.”
» that they said to you :: closely parallels 2 Peter 3:3's near-identical prediction — one of many verbal overlaps between Judah and 2 Peter's second chapter, longstanding evidence of a direct literary relationship between the two letters.
19  These are they who make separations, sensual, having not the Spirit.
---
20  But you all, beloved, building up yourselves on your most holy faith, praying in the Holy Spirit, [br]
21  keep yourselves in the love of God, looking for the mercy of our Lord Yeshua Messiah to eternal life.
---
22  And on some have mercy, who are in doubt; [br]
23  and some save, snatching them out of the fire; [br] and on some have mercy with fear; hating even the garment spotted by the flesh.
» and some save, snatching them out of the fire :: the Greek manuscript tradition varies significantly here (two groups vs. three, differing verbs) — one of the NT's more debated short textual cruxes; ASV's threefold structure represents one established critical reading, left unchanged.
>>>
24  Now to Him that is able to guard you from stumbling, and to set you before the presence of His glory without blemish in exceeding joy,
25  to the only God our Saviour, through Yeshua Messiah our Lord, be glory, majesty, dominion and power, before all time, and now, and for evermore. Amen.
» be glory, majesty, dominion and power :: a closing doxology in the shape of Jewish liturgical benedictions (cf. the closing blessings of the *Amidah*), stacking near-synonymous terms of praise — a form also found at Romans 11:36, 1 Timothy 1:17, Revelation 5:13.
