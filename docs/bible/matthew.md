# Matthew

## Matthew 1

1  The book of the generation of Yeshua Messiah, the son of David, the son of Abraham.[br]
2  Abraham fathered Isaac; and Isaac fathered Jacob; and Jacob fathered Judah and his brothers;[br]
3  and Judah fathered Perez and Zerah of Tamar; and Perez fathered Hezron; and Hezron fathered Ram;[br]
4  and Ram fathered Amminadab; and Amminadab fathered Nahshon; and Nahshon fathered Salmon;[br]
5  and Salmon fathered Boaz of Rahab; and Boaz fathered Obed of Ruth; and Obed fathered Jesse;[br]
6  and Jesse fathered David the king. And David fathered Solomon of her that had been the wife of Uriah;[br]
7  and Solomon fathered Rehoboam; and Rehoboam fathered Abijah; and Abijah fathered Asa;[br]
8  and Asa fathered Jehoshaphat; and Jehoshaphat fathered Joram; and Joram fathered Uzziah;[br]
9  and Uzziah fathered Jotham; and Jotham fathered Ahaz; and Ahaz fathered Hezekiah;[br]
10  and Hezekiah fathered Manasseh; and Manasseh fathered Amon; and Amon fathered Josiah;[br]
11  and Josiah fathered Jechoniah and his brothers, at the time of the carrying away to Babylon.[br]
» Jechoniah :: *Yekhonyah* (also Jeconiah, Coniah) — Jeremiah disqualifies his line from the throne: "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David" (Jeremiah 22:30). Matthew's genealogy runs through Jechoniah to Joseph; the Davidic covenant promise (2 Samuel 7:12–16) instead reaches Yeshua through Mary, whose own descent (Luke 3:23–31) runs through Nathan, another son of David, never passing through Solomon or Jechoniah.
12  And after the carrying away to Babylon, Jechoniah fathered Shealtiel; and Shealtiel fathered Zerubbabel;[br]
13  and Zerubbabel fathered Abiud; and Abiud fathered Eliakim; and Eliakim fathered Azor;[br]
14  and Azor fathered Sadoc; and Sadoc fathered Achim; and Achim fathered Eliud;[br]
15  and Eliud fathered Eleazar; and Eleazar fathered Matthan; and Matthan fathered Jacob;[br]
16  and Jacob fathered Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Yeshua, who is called Messiah.[br]
» of whom was born Yeshua :: *egennēsen* ("he fathered") runs the whole list — every other link is a man fathering a son — until this last one, which shifts without comment to a passive, *egennēthē* ("was born"), and to Mary as its object; the grammar itself, not a later comment, is where the text marks that Joseph is not Yeshua's father.
17  So all the generations from Abraham to David are fourteen generations;[br] and from David to the carrying away to Babylon fourteen generations;[br] and from the carrying away to Babylon to the Messiah fourteen generations.
» to the Messiah fourteen generations :: David's name in Hebrew consonants, *dvd* (dalet-vav-dalet = 4+6+4), sums to 14 — a gematria Matthew's three sets of fourteen generations spell out three times over, making "son of David" (v.1) the genealogy's real argument, not incidental framing.
===
18  Now the birth of Yeshua Messiah was in this way: When His mother Mary had been betrothed to Joseph, before they came together she was found with child of the Holy Spirit.[br]
19  And Joseph her husband, being a righteous man, and not willing to make her a public example, resolved to put her away secretly.[br]
20  But when he thought on these things, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream, saying, “Joseph, you son of David, fear not to take to you Mary your wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Spirit.
21  And she shall bring forth a son; and you shall call His name Yeshua; for it is He that shall save His people from their sins.”[br]
» you shall call His name Yeshua; for it is He that shall save His people from their sins :: *Yeshua* — the Second Temple contraction of *Yehoshua* ("YHWH saves"; the two forms interchange for the same men in Ezra 3:2/Zechariah 3:1 and Nehemiah 8:17). The clause plays on the name directly: *yoshia* ("he will save"), the verb used here, shares the same root *y-š-ʿ* as the name itself — name and verb are the same word doing two jobs in one sentence.
22  Now all this has happened, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the Lord through the prophet, saying,
23  “Behold, the virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, And they shall call His name Immanuel;” which is, being interpreted, God with us.
→ Behold, the virgin shall be with child :: Isaiah 7:14
24  And Joseph arose from his sleep, and did as the angel of the Lord commanded him, and took to him his wife;[br]
25  and knew her not till she had brought forth a son: and he called His name Yeshua.

## Matthew 2

1  Now when Yeshua was born in Bethlehem of Judea in the days of Herod the king, behold, Wise-men from the east came to Jerusalem, saying,[br]
2  “Where is He that is born King of the Jews? for we saw His star in the east, and have come to worship Him.”[br]
3  And when Herod the king heard it, he was troubled, and all Jerusalem with him.
4  And gathering together all the chief priests and scribes of the people, he inquired of them where the Messiah should be born.[br]
5  And they said to him, “In Bethlehem of Judea: for thus it is written through the prophet,
6  ‘And you Bethlehem, land of Judah,
      Are by no means least among the princes of Judah:
      For out of you shall come forth a governor,
      Who shall be shepherd of My people Israel.’”[br]
→ And you Bethlehem, land of Judah :: Micah 5:2
→ Who shall be shepherd of My people Israel :: 2 Samuel 5:2
» Are by no means least :: The Hebrew *tsa'ir* ("least, small") in Micah frames the paradox directly — the least of Judah's clans producing its greatest king; Matthew's citation adjusts the negative ("by no means least") to draw out that the smallness itself is now overturned.
7  Then Herod secretly called the Wise-men, and learned of them exactly what time the star appeared.
8  And he sent them to Bethlehem, and said, “Go and search out exactly concerning the young child; and when you all have found Him, bring me word, that I also may come and worship Him.”
---
9  And they, having heard the king, went their way; and behold, the star, which they saw in the east, went before them, till it came and stood over where the young child was.[br]
10  And when they saw the star, they rejoiced with exceedingly great joy.
11  And they came into the house and saw the young child with Mary His mother; and they fell down and worshipped Him; and opening their treasures they offered to Him gifts, gold and frankincense and myrrh.[br]
» gold and frankincense and myrrh :: Echoes Isaiah 60:6's vision of nations bringing gold and frankincense to Zion's rising light — the magi's homage reads against that prophetic expectation of Gentile tribute, with myrrh, an embalming spice, quietly anticipating the passion.
12  And being warned of God in a dream that they should not return to Herod, they departed into their own country another way.
---
13  Now when they were departed, behold, an angel of the Lord appears to Joseph in a dream, saying, “Arise and take the young child and His mother, and flee into Egypt, and be you there until I tell you: for Herod will seek the young child to destroy Him.”[br]
14  And he arose and took the young child and His mother by night, and departed into Egypt;
15  and was there until the death of Herod: that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the Lord through the prophet, saying, “Out of Egypt did I call My son.”[br]
→ Out of Egypt did I call My son :: Hosea 11:1
» Out of Egypt did I call My son :: In Hosea the phrase names Israel's own exodus, not a future prediction — "son" a title Israel first bore (Exodus 4:22); Matthew reads Yeshua's sojourn in Egypt as recapitulating the nation's own history, now embodied in one person.
---
16  Then Herod, when he saw that he was mocked of the Wise-men, was greatly enraged, and sent forth, and slew all the male children that were in Bethlehem, and in all its region, from two years old and under, according to the time which he had exactly learned of the Wise-men.
» slew all the male children that were in Bethlehem :: Unattested outside Matthew, but consistent with the Herod of Josephus, who executed three of his own sons on suspicion and, on his deathbed, ordered Judea's notables killed at his death to guarantee a nation's mourning.
17  Then was fulfilled that which was spoken through Jeremiah the prophet, saying,
18  “A voice was heard in Ramah,
      Weeping and great mourning,
      Rachel weeping for her children;
      And she would not be comforted, because they are not.”[br]
→ A voice was heard in Ramah :: Jeremiah 31:15
» Rachel weeping for her children :: Ramah, near Rachel's traditional tomb (Genesis 35:19), was the staging point for the Babylonian exile (Jeremiah 40:1) — Jeremiah's Rachel weeps for exiles who will return; Matthew applies the same image to a grief with no such return.
---
19  But when Herod was dead, behold, an angel of the Lord appears in a dream to Joseph in Egypt, saying,
20  “Arise and take the young child and His mother, and go into the land of Israel: for they are dead that sought the young child’s life.”[br]
21  And he arose and took the young child and His mother, and came into the land of Israel.
22  But when he heard that Archelaus was reigning over Judea in place of his father Herod, he was afraid to go there; and being warned of God in a dream, he withdrew into the parts of Galilee,
» Archelaus was reigning over Judea :: Archelaus's brutal early reign — Josephus records a Passover massacre in the Temple at his accession — made him notorious even by Herodian standards; Rome deposed him within a decade, but Joseph's fear was well founded.
23  and came and dwelt in a city called Nazareth; that it might be fulfilled which was spoken through the prophets, that He should be called a Nazarene.
» that He should be called a Nazarene :: No where in the Old Testament is there a references to Yeshua being a Nazarene.  It seems Matthew is summarizing multiple statements about the Messiah into a single statement.  Note that this is the only reference to "prophets" plural whereas previously in the chapter prophet is singular referencing a specific book.

## Matthew 3

1  And in those days comes John the Immerser, preaching in the wilderness of Judea, saying,[br]
2  “Repent you all; for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.”[br]
3  For this is he that was spoken of through Isaiah the prophet, saying, “The voice of one crying in the wilderness,
      Make you all ready the way of the Lord,
      Make His paths straight.”[br]
→ The voice of one crying in the wilderness :: Isaiah 40:3
4  Now John himself had his clothing of camel’s hair, and a leather belt around his waist; and his food was locusts and wild honey.
» a leather belt around his waist :: Echoes the description of Elijah in 2 Kings 1:8 — John's dress marks him as the returning Elijah of Malachi 4:5, made explicit later at 11:14 and 17:12–13.
5  Then went out to him Jerusalem, and all Judea, and all the region around the Jordan;[br]
6  and they were immersed of him in the river Jordan, confessing their sins.[br]
» confessing their sins :: *teshuvah* ("repentance, turning") — John's immersion draws on the existing Second Temple practice of ritual washing for purification (*mikveh*), but redirects it: not repeated ceremonial cleansing, but a one-time turning in view of the coming kingdom.
7  But when he saw many of the Pharisees and Sadducees coming to his immersion, he said to them, “You all offspring of vipers, who warned you to flee from the wrath to come?
» many of the Pharisees and Sadducees coming to his immersion :: Pharisees and Sadducees were natural rivals — Torah-zealous separatists versus the priestly-aristocratic establishment — their joint appearance here, paralleled by their joint opposition at 16:1, 6, marks John's movement as a concern crossing normally opposed factions.
8  Bring forth therefore fruit worthy of repentance:[br]
9  and think not to say within yourselves, ‘We have Abraham to our father’: for I say to you, that God is able of these stones to raise up children to Abraham.[br]
» God is able of these stones to raise up children to Abraham :: Aramaic wordplay — *avanim* ("stones") and *banim* ("children/sons") sound alike; John's saying puns on Israel's confidence in physical descent from Abraham.
10  And even now the axe lies at the root of the trees: every tree therefore that brings not forth good fruit is hewn down, and cast into the fire.[br]
11  I indeed immerse you in water to repentance: but He that comes after me is mightier than I, whose shoes I am not worthy to bear: He shall immerse you in the Holy Spirit and in fire:[br]
12  whose winnowing fork is in His hand, and He will thoroughly cleanse His threshing-floor; and He will gather His wheat into the barn, but the chaff He will burn up with unquenchable fire.”
---
13  Then comes Yeshua from Galilee to the Jordan to John, to be immersed of him.
14  But John would have hindered Him, saying, “I have need to be immersed of you, and come you to me?”[br]
15  But Yeshua answered and said to him, “Suffer it now: for thus it becomes us to fulfil all righteousness.” Then he suffers Him.[br]
» to fulfil all righteousness :: *tsedeq* ("righteousness") — not personal moral rectitude but full compliance with God's revealed order; Yeshua submits to a rite meant for sinners though sinless, identifying with the people he came to save.
16  And Yeshua, when He was immersed, went up immediately from the water: and behold, the heavens were opened to Him, and He saw the Spirit of God descending as a dove, and coming upon Him;[br]
17  and behold, a voice out of the heavens, saying, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.”[br]
» This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased :: fuses two Hebrew scriptures — the anointing of Psalm 2:7 ("today I have begotten you") and the Servant's commissioning in Isaiah 42:1 ("my chosen, in whom my soul delights"); the Father names Yeshua Messiah and suffering Servant in a single sentence.
→ This is My beloved Son :: Psalm 2:7
→ in whom I am well pleased :: Isaiah 42:1

## Matthew 4

1  Then was Yeshua led up by the Spirit into the wilderness to be tempted by the devil.
2  And when He had fasted forty days and forty nights, afterward He was hungry.
→ fasted forty days and forty nights :: Exodus 34:28
3  And the tempter came and said to Him, “If you are the Son of God, command that these stones become bread.”[br]
4  But He answered and said, “It is written, ‘Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds out of the mouth of God.’”[br]
→ Man shall not live by bread alone :: Deuteronomy 8:3
5  Then the devil takes Him into the holy city; and he set Him on the pinnacle of the temple,
6  and says to Him, “If you are the Son of God, cast yourself down: for it is written, ‘He shall give His angels charge concerning you: and, On their hands they shall bear you up, Lest perhaps you dash your foot against a stone.’”[br]
→ He shall give His angels charge concerning you :: Psalm 91:11–12
» He shall give His angels charge concerning you :: The devil quotes accurately but incompletely — Psalm 91 promises protection "in all your ways," i.e. paths of obedience, a qualifier the temptation silently drops.
7  Yeshua said to him, “Again it is written, ‘You shall not put the Lord your God to the test.’”[br]
→ You shall not put the Lord your God to the test :: Deuteronomy 6:16
» You shall not put the Lord your God to the test :: *ekpeirazō* ("to put thoroughly to the test") — an intensive compound of *peirazō*, the same root already rendered "tempted"/"the tempter" of the devil at v.1 and v.3. The two are distinct offenses sharing one Greek family: the devil's *peirazō* is enticement toward evil; this *ekpeirazō* is Yeshua's refusal to presumptuously test God, demanding He prove Himself — the same demand Israel made at Massah (Exodus 17:7, "they tested YHWH, saying, Is YHWH among us, or not?").
8  Again, the devil takes Him to an exceedingly high mountain, and shows Him all the kingdoms of the world, and the glory of them;
9  and he said to Him, “All these things I will give you, if you will fall down and worship me.”[br]
10  Then says Yeshua to him, “Away with you, Satan: for it is written, ‘You shall worship the Lord your God, and Him only shall you serve.’”[br]
→ You shall worship the Lord your God, and Him only shall you serve :: Deuteronomy 6:13
11  Then the devil leaves Him; and behold, angels came and ministered to Him.
→ Then the devil leaves Him :: James 4:7
» Then the devil leaves Him; and behold, angels came and ministered to Him :: All three replies come from Deuteronomy (8:3, 6:16, 6:13) — the book Israel failed to heed in forty years of wilderness wandering, now recited in a wilderness fast of forty days; where Israel failed each test (Exodus 16, 17; Deuteronomy 9), Yeshua succeeds.
---
12  Now when He heard that John was delivered up, He withdrew into Galilee;
13  and leaving Nazareth, He came and dwelt in Capernaum, which is by the sea, in the region of Zebulun and Naphtali:[br]
» Capernaum :: *Kefar Nahum* ("village of Nahum") — the Aramaic/Hebrew name behind the Greek *Kapharnaoum*; Yeshua's base of ministry in Galilee.
14  that it might be fulfilled which was spoken through Isaiah the prophet, saying,
15  “The land of Zebulun and the land of Naphtali,
      Toward the sea, beyond the Jordan, Galilee of the Gentiles,
→ The land of Zebulun and the land of Naphtali :: Isaiah 9:1–2
» Galilee of the Gentiles :: *Galil ha-Goyim* — Galilee's mixed population and proximity to Gentile territory made it, in the geography of Isaiah's own day, a byword for the nations pressing on Israel's borders; Matthew reads the light rising precisely where the darkness was thought deepest.
16  The people that sat in darkness
      Saw a great light,
      And to those who sat in the region and shadow of death,
      To them light dawned.”[br]
17  From that time Yeshua began to preach, and to say, “Repent you all; for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.”
→ Repent you all; for the kingdom of heaven is at hand :: Matthew 3:2
---
18  And walking by the sea of Galilee, He saw two brothers, Simon who is called Peter, and Andrew his brother, casting a net into the sea; for they were fishers.[br]
19  And He says to them, “Come you all after Me, and I will make you fishers of men.”[br]
» I will make you fishers of men :: A reversal of the same image in Jeremiah 16:16, where "fishers" gather Israel for judgment and exile — Yeshua repurposes the figure for a gathering unto the kingdom rather than a scattering from the land.
20  And they immediately left the nets, and followed Him.
21  And going on from there He saw two other brothers, Jacob the son of Zebedee, and John his brother, in the boat with Zebedee their father, mending their nets; and He called them.[br]
22  And they immediately left the boat and their father, and followed Him.
---
23  And Yeshua went about in all Galilee, teaching in their synagogues, and preaching the good news of the kingdom, and healing every kind of disease and every kind of sickness among the people.
24  And the report of Him went forth into all Syria: and they brought to Him all that were sick, afflicted with various diseases and torments, possessed with demons, and epileptic, and paralyzed; and He healed them.[br]
25  And great multitudes followed Him from Galilee and Decapolis and Jerusalem and Judea and from beyond the Jordan.

## Matthew 5

---
1  And seeing the multitudes, He went up into the mountain: and when He had sat down, His disciples came to Him:
2  and He opened His mouth and taught them, saying,[br]
» He opened His mouth :: *patach et-piv* ("he opened his mouth") — a Hebrew idiom introducing solemn, weighty speech (Job 3:1; Psalm 78:2, quoted at Matthew 13:35).
3  “Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.[br]
» Blessed are :: *ashrei* behind the Greek *makarioi* — the same opening word as Psalm 1:1's "blessed is the man"; the Beatitudes read as a fresh interpretation of the Torah given from the promised prophet of Deuteronomy 18, reinvigorating the original intent of the Law that had been overgrown by the oral tradition.
4  Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted.[br]
→ they shall be comforted :: Isaiah 61:2
5  Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth.[br]
→ they shall inherit the earth :: Psalm 37:11
6  Blessed are they that hunger and thirst for righteousness: for they shall be filled.[br]
7  Blessed are the merciful: for they shall obtain mercy.[br]
8  Blessed are the pure in heart: for they shall see God.[br]
→ pure in heart :: Psalm 24:4
9  Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called sons of God.[br]
10  Blessed are they that have been persecuted for righteousness’ sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.[br]
11  Blessed are you all when men shall reproach you all, and persecute you all, and say every kind of evil against you all falsely, for My sake.
12  Rejoice, and be exceedingly glad: for great is your reward in heaven: for so they persecuted the prophets that were before you.[br]
13  You all are the salt of the earth: but if the salt have lost its savor, with what shall it be salted? it is from then on good for nothing, but to be cast out and trodden under foot by men.[br]
» salt of the earth :: Salts use in the Torah was covenantal, not culinary — the "covenant of salt" sealing priestly and Davidic grants (Numbers 18:19, 2 Chronicles 13:5); the image casts the disciples' role as a preserving, covenant-marking presence.
14  You all are the light of the world. A city set on a hill cannot be hid.
15  Neither do men light a lamp, and put it under the bushel, but on the stand; and it shines to all that are in the house.
16  Even so let your light shine before men; that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven.[br]
17  Think not that I came to destroy the Torah or the prophets: I came not to destroy, but to fulfill.[br]
» the Torah or the prophets :: *Torah v'Nevi'im* — the two-part shorthand for the whole Hebrew canon current in Second Temple usage (cf. 7:12, 22:40); Yeshua positions himself not against Torah but as its intended fulfillment.
18  For truly I say to you, Till heaven and earth pass away, one jot or one tittle shall by no means pass away from the Torah, till all things be accomplished.[br]
» one jot or one tittle :: *yod*, the smallest Hebrew letter, and the *tagin*, the decorative crowns on certain letters in a Torah scroll — a scribal-precision idiom for the law's smallest strokes, not a figure of speech invented for the occasion.
19  Whoever therefore shall break one of these least commandments, and shall teach men so, shall be called least in the kingdom of heaven: but whoever shall do and teach them, he shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven.[br]
20  For I say to you, that except your righteousness shall exceed the righteousness of the scribes and Pharisees, you all shall by no means enter into the kingdom of heaven.[br]
21  You all have heard that it was said to the ancients, ‘You shall not kill’; and whoever shall kill shall be liable to the judgment:
→ You shall not kill :: Exodus 20:13
22  but I say to you, that every one who is angry with his brother shall be liable to the judgment; and whoever shall say to his brother, ‘Raca,’ shall be liable to the council; and whoever shall say, ‘You fool,’ shall be liable to the Gehenna of fire.[br]
» Raca :: Aramaic term of contempt ("empty-head, worthless one") — the escalating triad of anger, insult, and curse is matched to an escalating triad of tribunals, local judgment, the Sanhedrin, Gehenna.
» the Gehenna of fire :: *Gehenna* — the Valley of Hinnom outside Jerusalem, site of child sacrifice under the monarchy (2 Kings 23:10) and, by the Second Temple period, the fixed image for final judgment.
23  If therefore you are offering your gift at the altar, and there remember that your brother has anything against you,
24  leave there your gift before the altar, and go your way, first be reconciled to your brother, and then come and offer your gift.[br]
25  Agree with your adversary quickly, while you are with him on the way; lest perhaps the adversary deliver you to the judge, and the judge deliver you to the officer, and you be cast into prison.
26  Truly I say to you, You shall by no means come out from there, till you have paid the last quadrans.[br]
» quadrans :: A Roman copper coin — the smallest denomination in common use, the same coin behind the widow's offering of Mark 12:42.
27  You all have heard that it was said, ‘You shall not commit adultery’:
→ You shall not commit adultery :: Exodus 20:14
28  but I say to you, that every one that looks on a woman to lust after her has committed adultery with her already in his heart.[br]
» to lust after her :: *epithymeō* ("to desire, covet") — the verb the LXX uses for *tachmod* ("you shall covet") in the tenth commandment (Exodus 20:17; Deuteronomy 5:21), which names a neighbor's wife; a verb of intent, not of act.
29  And if your right eye causes you to stumble, pluck it out, and cast it from you: for it is to your advantage that one part of your body should perish, and not your whole body be cast into Gehenna.[br]
30  And if your right hand causes you to stumble, cut it off, and cast it from you: for it is to your advantage that one part of your body should perish, and not your whole body go into Gehenna.[br]
31  It was said also, ‘Whoever shall divorce his wife, let him give her a certificate of divorce’:
→ let him give her a certificate of divorce :: Deuteronomy 24:1
» let him give her a certificate of divorce :: A live halakhic dispute of the day: Beit Shammai read Deuteronomy 24:1's "unseemly thing" narrowly, as grave misconduct; Beit Hillel broadly, even a ruined meal (m. Gittin 9:10) — Yeshua narrows the ground to fornication, nearer Shammai's reading but grounded in the marriage bond itself (19:4–6), not in halakhic precedent.
32  but I say to you, that every one that divorces his wife, except for the cause of fornication, makes her an adulteress: and whoever shall marry her when she is divorced commits adultery.[br]
33  Again, you all have heard that it was said to the ancients, ‘You shall not swear falsely, but shall fulfill your oaths to the Lord’:
→ You shall not swear falsely :: Leviticus 19:12
34  but I say to you, Swear not at all; neither by the heaven, for it is the throne of God;[br]
» neither by the heaven, for it is the throne of God :: Mishnaic oath-law (m. Shevuot 4:13, m. Nedarim 1:3) already debated which formulas of swearing "by" something bound as fully as swearing by the Name itself — Yeshua cuts through the whole casuistry of graduated oaths rather than adjudicating within it.
35  nor by the earth, for it is the footstool of His feet; nor by Jerusalem, for it is the city of the great King.[br]
→ the footstool of His feet :: Isaiah 66:1
→ the city of the great King :: Psalm 48:2
36  Neither shall you swear by your head, for you cannot make one hair white or black.
37  But let your speech be, ‘Yes, yes; No, no’: and whatever is more than these is of the evil one.[br]
» Yes, yes; No, no :: The doubled affirmation has a rabbinic parallel in the exposition "let your *hen* ('yes') be right and your *lav* ('no') be right" (cf. b. Bava Metzia 49a), plain speech held to the standard of an oath.
38  You all have heard that it was said, ‘An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth’:
→ An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth :: Exodus 21:24
39  but I say to you, Resist him not that is evil: but whoever strikes you on your right cheek, turn to him the other also.
40  And if any man would go to law with you, and take away your tunic, let him have your cloak also.
» let him have your cloak also :: *himation* ("outer cloak") — the garment the Torah forbids a creditor to keep overnight as a pledge, "for that is his only covering" (Exodus 22:26–27; Deuteronomy 24:12–13).
41  And whoever shall compel you to go one mile, go with him two.
» whoever shall compel you to go one mile :: *angareia* — the legal right of Roman authority to press a civilian into forced service (the same term behind Simon of Cyrene's conscription at 27:32); Yeshua turns a resented occupation-law obligation into an occasion for voluntary excess.
42  Give to him that asks you, and from him that would borrow from you do not turn away.[br]
43  You all have heard that it was said, ‘You shall love your neighbor, and hate your enemy’:
→ You shall love your neighbor :: Leviticus 19:18
» hate your enemy :: Leviticus 19:18 commands love of neighbor but nowhere commands hatred of an enemy — that clause reflects a popular extension of the text (cf. the Qumran community's own rule to "hate the sons of darkness," 1QS 1:10), which Yeshua explicitly rejects rather than the Torah text itself.
44  but I say to you, Love your enemies, and pray for those that persecute you all;
45  that you all may be sons of your Father who is in heaven: for He makes His sun to rise on the evil and the good, and sends rain on the just and the unjust.
46  For if you all love them that love you all, what reward have you all?  Do not even the tax collectors the same?
47  And if you all greet your brothers only, what do you all more than others?  Do not even the Gentiles the same?
48  You all therefore shall be complete, as your heavenly Father is complete.”
» You all therefore shall be complete :: *teleios* ("complete, whole") — LXX rendering of *tamim* (Deuteronomy 18:13, "You shall be *tamim* with YHWH your God"; cf. Genesis 6:9, 17:1); the future "shall be" carries the force of the Hebrew imperative.

## Matthew 6

1  “Take heed that you all do not practice your righteousness before men, to be seen by them: else you all have no reward with your Father who is in heaven.

» the hypocrites :: *hypokritai*, "hypocrites," originally named stage actors performing behind a mask — a fitting image for righteousness staged for an audience.
3  But when you give alms, let not your left hand know what your right hand does:
4  that your alms may be in secret: and your Father who sees in secret shall reward you.[br]

» They have received their reward :: *apechō* ("to receive in full") — the standard term on first-century receipts for a payment acknowledged as settled; the formula repeats at 6:2, 6:16.
6  But you, when you pray, enter into your inner chamber, and having shut your door, pray to your Father who is in secret, and your Father who sees in secret shall reward you.[br]
7  And in praying use not vain repetitions, as the Gentiles do: for they think that they shall be heard for their many words.
» use not vain repetitions, as the Gentiles do :: *battalogeō* — likely echoing pagan incantation, piling up divine names and formulas to compel a hearing; the brevity of the prayer that follows is itself the contrast.
8  Therefore do not be like them: for your Father knows what things you all have need of, before you all ask Him.

10  Your kingdom come. Your will be done, as in heaven, so on earth.
» Your kingdom come :: *yamlich malkhuteh* ("may He establish His kingdom")
11  Give us this day our daily bread.
» our daily bread :: *epiousios*, translated "daily," is rare in surviving Greek outside this prayer — plausibly "bread for the coming day," echoing the manna's one-day provision (Exodus 16:4) rather than a general request for sustenance.
12  And forgive us our debts, as we also have forgiven our debtors.[br]
» our debts :: Aramaic commonly used "debt" (*chovah*) as a metaphor for sin; the prayer makes the disciple's own forgiveness of others the explicit measure of the petition, unpacked at once in v.14–15.
13  And bring us not into testing, but deliver us from the evil one.’[br]

» disfigure their faces :: Public fasting and mourning were marked by sackcloth, ash, and the omission of anointing (cf. Daniel 9:3; 2 Samuel 14:2) — the hypocrite exaggerates these very marks of piety rather than concealing them, inverting the point of the fast.

» Father who is in secret :: An inversion of praying in secret referenced a few verses earlier, God the Father sees both praying in secret and what is done publicly by holding in secret a personal fast.

» mammon :: Aramaic *mamona* ("wealth, property") — left untranslated because no single Greek or English word carried its connotation of wealth as a rival claimant to trust, treated here almost as a personified competing master.

» add one cubit to the measure of his life :: a length-measure applied to time, as in Psalm 39:5, "You have made my days handbreadths."

→ clothe the grass of the field :: Isaiah 40:6–8
» O you of little faith :: *oligopistoi* — an Aramaic/rabbinic idiom (*ketanei amanah*): b. Sotah 48b applies it to one who, with bread in his basket, asks "What shall I eat tomorrow?" — the very anxiety of vv.25–34. First occurrence in Matthew (cf. 8:26, 14:31, 16:8).

» Give not that which is holy to the dogs :: Sacrificial meat (*basar kodesh*) was restricted to those in a state of ritual fitness (cf. ## Matthew 28

---
1  Now late on the sabbath day, as it began to dawn toward the first day of the week, came Mary Magdalene and the other Mary to see the tomb.
2  And behold, there was a great earthquake; for an angel of the Lord descended from heaven, and came and rolled away the stone, and sat upon it.[br]
3  His appearance was as lightning, and his clothing white as snow:[br]
4  and for fear of him the watchers did quake, and became as dead men.
5  And the angel answered and said to the women, “Fear not you all; for I know that you all seek Yeshua, who has been crucified.[br]
6  He is not here; for He is risen, even as He said. Come, see the place where the Lord lay.[br]
7  And go quickly, and tell His disciples, ‘He is risen from the dead; and behold, He goes before you into Galilee; there shall you all see Him’: behold, I have told you.”[br]
8  And they departed quickly from the tomb with fear and great joy, and ran to bring his disciples word.
---
9  And behold, Yeshua met them, saying, “All hail.” And they came and took hold of his feet, and worshipped him.[br]
» took hold of his feet, and worshipped him :: *proskynesis* — full prostration, grasping the feet, was the posture of homage before a king or, in a religious context, before God; the women's spontaneous gesture registers something beyond ordinary greeting.
10  Then says Yeshua to them, “Fear not: go tell my brothers that they depart into Galilee, and there shall they see me.”
---
11  Now while they were going, behold, some of the guard came into the city, and told to the chief priests all the things that had happened.
12  And when they were assembled with the elders, and had taken counsel, they gave much money to the soldiers,[br]
13  saying, “Say you all, ‘His disciples came by night, and stole him away while we slept.’[br]
14  And if this come to the governor’s ears, we will persuade him, and rid you of care.”[br]
15  So they took the money, and did as they were taught: and this saying was spread abroad among the Jews, and continues until this day.
---
16  But the eleven disciples went into Galilee, to the mountain where Yeshua had appointed them.
17  And when they saw him, they worshipped him; but some doubted.
18  And Yeshua came to them and spoke to them, saying, “All authority has been given to me in heaven and on earth.[br]
19  Go you all therefore, and make disciples of all the nations, immersing them into the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit:[br]
» Go you all therefore, and make disciples of all the nations :: Reverses the earlier restriction of the mission "to the lost sheep of the house of Israel" (10:6) — the same Jewish teacher who once bounded his and his disciples' mission to Israel now sends them to all nations, the promise made to Abraham (Genesis 12:3) reaching its intended scope.
20  teaching them to observe all things whatever I commanded you: and behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the world.”
