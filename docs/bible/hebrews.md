# Hebrews

## Hebrews 1

1  God, having of old time spoken to the fathers in the prophets by many portions and in many manners,
2  has at the end of these days spoken to us in His Son, whom He appointed heir of all things, [br] through whom also He made the worlds;
» at the end of these days :: *acharit ha-yamim* ("the latter days") — the fixed prophetic horizon-formula (Genesis 49:1; Numbers 24:14; Isaiah 2:2; Daniel 10:14); the author opens by declaring that horizon reached.
3  who, being the effulgence of His glory, and the exact imprint of His nature, and upholding all things by the word of His power, [br] when He had made purification of sins, sat down on the right hand of the Majesty on high;
» the exact imprint of His nature :: *charaktēr tēs hypostaseōs* — a die-stamp, the impression struck into a coin by its engraved die, not a mere resemblance (*eikōn* would be "likeness"); *hypostasis* is the underlying reality itself. The pair claims the Son bears God's very essence stamped in full.
» the right hand of the Majesty on high :: *tēs megalōsynēs* ("the Majesty") — a reverential surrogate for the Name itself, alongside the rabbis' *HaGevurah* ("the Power") and *HaMakom* ("the Place"); the author circles the Name as a Jew trained to.
4  having become by so much better than the angels, as He has inherited a more excellent name than they.
---
5  For to which of the angels said He at any time, [br] “You are my Son, This day have I begotten you”? [br] And again, “I will be to Him a Father, And He shall be to me a Son”?
→ This day have I begotten you :: Psalm 2:7
→ He shall be to me a Son :: 2 Samuel 7:14
6  And again, when He brings the firstborn into the world, He says, [br] “And let all the angels of God worship Him.”
→ let all the angels of God worship Him :: Deuteronomy 32:43 (LXX) / Psalm 97:7
» the firstborn :: *bekhor* — a rank of inheritance and preeminence, not birth order (Psalm 89:27, "I will make him my firstborn, the highest of the kings of the earth"); the title claims the Son's standing, not His origin.
7  And of the angels He says, [br] “Who makes His angels winds, [br] and His ministers a flame of fire”:
→ Who makes His angels winds :: Psalm 104:4
8  but of the Son He says, [br] “Your throne, O God, is forever and ever; [br] and the sceptre of uprightness is the sceptre of your kingdom.
→ Your throne, O God, is forever and ever :: Psalm 45:6
» Your throne, O God :: Psalm 45:6's Hebrew (*kis'akha Elohim*) reads either as address ("your throne, O God") or predicate ("your throne is God('s)"); the LXX and the author take it as address — the king hailed as *Elohim*.
9  You have loved righteousness, and hated iniquity; [br] therefore God, your God, has anointed you [br] with the oil of gladness above your fellows.”
→ therefore God, your God, has anointed you :: Psalm 45:7
---
10  And, “You, Lord, in the beginning did lay the foundation of the earth, [br] And the heavens are the works of your hands:
→ You, Lord, in the beginning did lay the foundation of the earth :: Psalm 102:25
» You, Lord, in the beginning :: Psalm 102 addresses YHWH directly (v.1, "O YHWH, hear my prayer"); the author applies its ascription of eternal, unaging creatorship to the Son without comment — the identification is assumed, not argued.
11  They shall perish; but you continue: [br] And they all shall grow old as does a garment;
12  And as a mantle shall you roll them up, [br] As a garment, and they shall be changed: [br] But you are the same, And your years shall not fail.”
→ your years shall not fail :: Psalm 102:27
13  But of which of the angels has He said at any time, [br] “Sit you on my right hand, [br] Till I make your enemies the footstool of your feet”?
→ Sit you on my right hand :: Psalm 110:1
» Sit you on my right hand :: Psalm 110:1's *ne'um YHWH l'Adoni* ("YHWH's oracle to my lord") puzzled the rabbis enough to become the classic text behind the "two powers in heaven" debate (b. Sanhedrin 38b); it is also the single most-quoted Old Testament verse in the New.
>>>
14  Are they not all ministering spirits, sent forth to do service for the sake of them that shall inherit salvation?

## Hebrews 2

1  Therefore we ought to give the more earnest heed to the things that were heard, [br] lest perhaps we drift away from them.
2  For if the word spoken through angels proved steadfast, [br] and every transgression and disobedience received a just recompense of reward;
3  how shall we escape, if we neglect so great a salvation? [br] which having at the first been spoken through the Lord, was confirmed to us by them that heard;
» how shall we escape :: *kal va-chomer* ("light and heavy") — the first of Hillel's seven exegetical rules, arguing from the lesser case to the greater; if a word spoken through angels carried a just penalty, how much graver to neglect one spoken through the Lord Himself. The pattern recurs through the letter (9:13-14; 10:28-29).
4  God also bearing witness with them, both by signs and wonders, and by manifold powers, [br] and by gifts of the Holy Spirit, according to His own will.
---
5  For not to angels did He subject the world to come, of which we speak.
6  But one has somewhere testified, saying, [br] “What is man, that you are mindful of him? [br] Or the son of man, that you visit him?
→ What is man, that you are mindful of him :: Psalm 8:4
» the son of man :: Hebrew *ben adam* — in Psalm 8 a plain idiom for "a human being" (as often in Ezekiel's address), not yet the titular "Son of Man" of Daniel 7:13; the ordinary phrase carries the weight of the extraordinary application in v.9.
7  You made him a little lower than the angels; [br] You crowned him with glory and honor, [br] And did set him over the works of your hands:
→ You made him a little lower than the angels :: Psalm 8:5
8  You did put all things in subjection under his feet.” [br] For in that He subjected all things to him, He left nothing that is not subject to him. [br] But now we see not yet all things subjected to him.
→ You did put all things in subjection under his feet :: Psalm 8:6
9  But we behold Him who has been made a little lower than the angels, even Yeshua, [br] because of the suffering of death crowned with glory and honor, that by the grace of God He should taste of death for every man.
» even Yeshua :: The author reads Psalm 8's dominion of "the son of man" first as Adam's unfulfilled mandate (Genesis 1:26-28 — "not yet all things subjected," v.8) and then as fulfilled in Yeshua: a Last-Adam typology Paul will later name outright (1 Corinthians 15:45).
---
10  For it became Him, for whom are all things, and through whom are all things, [br] in bringing many sons to glory, to make the author of their salvation perfect through sufferings.
» the author of their salvation :: *archēgos* ("pioneer / captain / originator") — the one who goes first and breaks the trail for those who follow; the same word returns at 12:2, "the author and perfecter of our faith."
11  For both He that sanctifies and they that are sanctified are all of one: [br] for which cause He is not ashamed to call them brothers,
12  saying, [br] “I will declare your name to my brothers, [br] In the midst of the congregation will I sing your praise.”
→ In the midst of the congregation will I sing your praise :: Psalm 22:22
» In the midst of the congregation will I sing your praise :: Psalm 22:22 — the pivot-verse of the crucifixion psalm, where the sufferer's voice turns from lament to praise; Hebrews hears the risen Messiah Himself leading the *qahal*, Israel's assembled worship, as one of the brothers.
13  And again, “I will put my trust in Him.” [br] And again, “Behold, I and the children whom God has given me.”
→ I will put my trust in Him :: Isaiah 8:17
→ Behold, I and the children whom God has given me :: Isaiah 8:18
>>>
14  Since then the children are sharers in flesh and blood, He also Himself in like manner partook of the same; [br] that through death He might bring to nought him that had the power of death, that is, the devil;
» him that had the power of death :: The *Malach ha-Mavet* ("Angel of Death") of rabbinic tradition personifies what the author names outright — the devil's hold on death itself, rendered powerless (*katargēsē*) rather than merely evaded.
15  and might deliver all them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage.
16  For truly not to angels does He give help, [br] but He gives help to the seed of Abraham.
17  Therefore it was fitting for Him in all things to be made like to His brothers, [br] that He might become a merciful and faithful high priest in things pertaining to God, to make propitiation for the sins of the people.
» to make propitiation for the sins of the people :: *hilaskesthai* renders Hebrew *kipper* ("to atone / cover"), the Yom Kippur verb (Leviticus 16); the high-priest argument this letter develops fully in chapters 7-10 is already seeded here.
18  For in that He Himself has suffered being tempted, He is able to succor them that are tempted.

Berakhot 5:5), and the One who represents Israel back to God.

→ Today if you all shall hear His voice :: Psalm 95:7
8  Harden not your hearts, as in the provocation, [br] Like as in the day of the trial in the wilderness,
→ Harden not your hearts, as in the provocation :: Psalm 95:8

→ your fathers tried me by proving me :: Psalm 95:9
10  Therefore I was displeased with this generation, [br] And said, They do always err in their heart: [br] But they did not know my ways;

## Hebrews 4

1  Let us fear therefore, lest perhaps, a promise being left of entering into His rest, [br] any one of you should seem to have come short of it.
2  For indeed we have had good tidings preached to us, even as also they: [br] but the word of hearing did not profit them, because it was not united by faith with them that heard.
---
3  For we who have believed do enter into that rest; even as He has said, [br] “As I swore in my wrath, [br] They shall not enter into my rest”: [br] although the works were finished from the foundation of the world.
→ They shall not enter into my rest :: Psalm 95:11
4  For He has said somewhere of the seventh day in this way, [br] “And God rested on the seventh day from all His works”;
→ God rested on the seventh day from all His works :: Genesis 2:2
5  and in this place again, [br] “They shall not enter into my rest.”
6  Seeing therefore it remains that some should enter thereinto, [br] and they to whom the good tidings were before preached failed to enter in because of disobedience,
7  He again defines a certain day, Today, saying in David so long a time afterward (even as has been said before), [br] “Today if you all shall hear His voice, [br] Harden not your hearts.”
→ Today if you all shall hear His voice :: Psalm 95:7
8  For if Joshua had given them rest, He would not have spoken afterward of another day.
9  There remains therefore a sabbath rest for the people of God.
» a sabbath rest :: *sabbatismos* — a term apparently coined for this verse (its only NT occurrence), built on *shabbat*; Israel's weekly sign points to a rest the wilderness generation never entered, echoing the Sabbath's place at creation itself (Genesis 2:2-3).
10  For he that is entered into His rest has himself also rested from his works, [br] as God did from His.
11  Let us therefore give diligence to enter into that rest, that no man fall after the same example of disobedience.
>>>
12  For the word of God is living, and active, and sharper than any two-edged sword, [br] and piercing even to the dividing of soul and spirit, of both joints and marrow, [br] and quick to discern the thoughts and intents of the heart.
» the word of God is living, and active :: *davar* — in the Hebrew Bible God's word is never inert speech but active force (Isaiah 55:11, "My word...shall not return to me void"); the author personifies it further as a two-edged sword, echoing the sword-like tongue of Isaiah 49:2.
13  And there is no creature that is not manifest in His sight: [br] but all things are naked and laid open before the eyes of Him with whom we have to do.
» naked and laid open :: *tetrachēlismena* — a term from the sacrificial altar, the animal's throat bared and exposed for the priest's inspection; the imagery folds into the letter's larger priestly argument.
>>>
14  Having then a great high priest, who has passed through the heavens, Yeshua the Son of God, [br] let us hold fast our confession.
» who has passed through the heavens :: On Yom Kippur the earthly high priest passed through one veil into the Holy of Holies once a year; here Yeshua passes through the heavens themselves — the ultimate, once-only entry the earthly ritual only rehearsed (developed fully in ch. 9).
15  For we have not a high priest that cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; [br] but one that has been in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.
16  Let us therefore draw near with boldness to the throne of grace, [br] that we may receive mercy, and may find grace to help us in time of need.

## Hebrews 5

1  For every high priest, being taken from among men, is appointed for men in things pertaining to God, [br] that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins:
2  who can bear gently with the ignorant and erring, [br] for that he himself also is compassed with infirmity;
3  and by reason thereof is bound, as for the people, so also for himself, to offer for sins.
4  And no man takes the honor to himself, [br] but when he is called of God, even as was Aaron.
» even as was Aaron :: A pointed contrast for a Second-Temple audience, where the high priesthood had for over a century been bought, appointed by Rome, and stripped from the Zadokite line; the author insists the office is God's call to give, not a man's to seize.
---
5  So Messiah also glorified not Himself to be made a high priest, [br] but He that spoke to Him, [br] “You are my Son, This day have I begotten you”:
→ This day have I begotten you :: Psalm 2:7
6  as He says also in another place, [br] “You are a priest for ever [br] After the order of Melchizedek.”
→ You are a priest for ever :: Psalm 110:4
7  Who in the days of His flesh, having offered up prayers and supplications with strong crying and tears to Him that was able to save Him from death, [br] and having been heard for His godly fear,
» having been heard for His godly fear :: Not spared from death but vindicated through it — the prayer of Gethsemane ("not what I will, but what You will," Mark 14:36) answered by resurrection rather than rescue.
8  though He was a Son, yet learned obedience by the things which He suffered;
9  and having been made perfect, He became to all them that obey Him the author of eternal salvation;
10  named of God a high priest after the order of Melchizedek.
>>>
11  Of whom we have many things to say, and hard of interpretation, [br] seeing you all are become dull of hearing.
12  For when by reason of the time you all ought to be teachers, you all have need again that some one teach you the rudiments of the first principles of the oracles of God; [br] and are become such as have need of milk, and not of solid food.
13  For every one that partakes of milk is without experience of the word of righteousness; [br] for he is a babe.
14  But solid food is for fullgrown men, even those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern good and evil.
» fullgrown men :: *teleios* — the same root rendered "made perfect" of Messiah Himself in v.9; the disciple's maturity is named with the very word used of the Master's own perfecting through what He suffered.

## Hebrews 6

1  Therefore leaving the doctrine of the first principles of Messiah, let us press on to perfection; [br] not laying again a foundation of repentance from dead works, and of faith toward God,
» the first principles of Messiah :: The six foundations named here (repentance, faith, immersions, laying on of hands, resurrection, eternal judgment) read as a catechism drawn from Second-Temple Jewish instruction more than from distinctly new doctrine; the complaint is that the readers still need the entry-level curriculum they share with the synagogue.
2  of the teaching of immersions, and of laying on of hands, [br] and of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.
» immersions :: Plural — *tevilot*, the repeated ritual washings of Jewish practice (cf. mikveh use), not yet the single, once-for-all immersion into Messiah the letter elsewhere assumes; the two are easy to conflate and the author's readers would have needed both distinguished.
3  And this will we do, if God permit.
---
4  For as touching those who were once enlightened and tasted of the heavenly gift, [br] and were made partakers of the Holy Spirit,
5  and tasted the good word of God, and the powers of the age to come,
6  and then fell away, it is impossible to renew them again to repentance; [br] seeing they crucify to themselves the Son of God afresh, and put Him to an open shame.
7  For the land which has drunk the rain that comes oft upon it, and brings forth herbs meet for them for whose sake it is also tilled, receives blessing from God:
8  but if it bears thorns and thistles, it is rejected and near to a curse; whose end is to be burned.
---
9  But, beloved, we are persuaded better things of you, [br] and things that accompany salvation, though we thus speak:
10  for God is not unrighteous to forget your work and the love which you all showed toward His name, [br] in that you all ministered to the saints, and still do minister.
11  And we desire that each one of you may show the same diligence to the fulness of hope even to the end:
12  that you all be not sluggish, but imitators of them who through faith and patience inherit the promises.
---
13  For when God made promise to Abraham, since He could swear by none greater, He swore by Himself,
» For when God made promise to Abraham :: The same episode as the *Akedah*, the Binding of Isaac (Genesis 22) — Abraham "having offered up his only son" is named outright at 11:17; the sacrificial background of this oath is not incidental to a letter arguing for one final, greater offering.
14  saying, [br] “Surely blessing I will bless you, and multiplying I will multiply you.”
→ Surely blessing I will bless you, and multiplying I will multiply you :: Genesis 22:17
15  And thus, having patiently endured, he obtained the promise.
16  For men swear by the greater: [br] and in every dispute of theirs the oath is final for confirmation.
17  In which God, being minded to show more abundantly to the heirs of the promise the immutability of His counsel, [br] interposed with an oath;
18  that by two immutable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie, we may have a strong encouragement, [br] who have fled for refuge to lay hold of the hope set before us:
19  which we have as an anchor of the soul, a hope both sure and steadfast and entering into that which is within the veil;
20  where as a forerunner Yeshua entered for us, having become a high priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek.
» as a forerunner :: *prodromos* — a scout or advance-runner; elsewhere in Greek usage, a ship sent ahead to test a harbor's waters. Its only NT occurrence, and the bridge straight into ch. 7's Melchizedek argument.

## Hebrews 7

1  For this Melchizedek, king of Salem, priest of God Most High, [br] who met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him,
» God Most High :: *El Elyon* — first named at Melchizedek's own introduction (Genesis 14:18-20), a title used across the ancient Near East for the supreme god, here claimed for YHWH alone through the blessing Melchizedek pronounces over Abraham.
→ who met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him :: Genesis 14:18-20
2  to whom also Abraham divided a tenth part of all (being first, by interpretation, King of righteousness, [br] and then also King of Salem, which is, King of peace;
» King of righteousness :: *Malki-Tzedek* ("king of righteousness") and *Shalem* ("peace" — also Jerusalem's ancient name): the author is doing real Hebrew etymology, exactly as a rabbinic *derash* would, reading the priest-king's name and city as a two-word sermon joining righteousness to peace in one office.
3  without father, without mother, without genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life, [br] but made like to the Son of God), abides a priest continually.
» without father, without mother, without genealogy :: An argument from scriptural silence: Genesis records no birth, death, or lineage for Melchizedek, and the author reads that silence as deliberate pattern — not that Melchizedek was himself eternal, but that Scripture presents him as if he were, "made like" the One he prefigures.
---
4  Now consider how great this man was, [br] to whom Abraham, the patriarch, gave a tenth out of the chief spoils.
5  And they indeed of the sons of Levi that receive the priest's office have commandment to take tithes of the people according to the Torah, [br] that is, of their brothers, though these have come out of the loins of Abraham:
6  but he whose genealogy is not counted from them has taken tithes of Abraham, [br] and has blessed him that has the promises.
7  But without any dispute the less is blessed of the better.
8  And here men that die receive tithes; but there one, of whom it is witnessed that he lives.
9  And, so to say, through Abraham even Levi, who receives tithes, has paid tithes;
10  for he was yet in the loins of his father, when Melchizedek met him.
» for he was yet in the loins of his father :: A rabbinic-style argument from federal representation: Levi is reckoned to have already "paid" tithes because he was, in a real legal sense, present in Abraham's body — the same logic of corporate solidarity that underlies the Adam/Messiah argument at Romans 5:12-19.
---
11  Now if there was perfection through the Levitical priesthood (for under it has the people received the Torah), [br] what further need was there that another priest should arise after the order of Melchizedek, and not be reckoned after the order of Aaron?
12  For the priesthood being changed, [br] there is made of necessity a change also of the Torah.
13  For He of whom these things are said belongs to another tribe, [br] from which no man has given attendance at the altar.
14  For it is evident that our Lord has sprung out of Judah; [br] as to which tribe Moses spoke nothing concerning priests.
» our Lord has sprung out of Judah :: A live objection for any Jewish reader: Torah reserves the priesthood to Levi (Numbers 3:10), and Yeshua's Davidic, Judahite lineage would ordinarily disqualify Him from priesthood entirely — which is exactly the author's point: His priesthood is of a wholly different order.
15  And what we say is yet more abundantly evident, if after the likeness of Melchizedek there arises another priest,
16  who has been made, not after the Torah of a carnal commandment, [br] but after the power of an endless life:
17  for it is witnessed of Him, [br] “You are a priest for ever [br] After the order of Melchizedek.”
→ You are a priest for ever :: Psalm 110:4
18  For there is a disannulling of a foregoing commandment because of its weakness and unprofitableness
19  (for the Torah made nothing perfect), [br] and a bringing in thereupon of a better hope, through which we draw near to God.
---
20  And inasmuch as it is not without the taking of an oath
21  (for they indeed have been made priests without an oath; [br] but He with an oath by Him that says of Him, [br] “The Lord swore and will not repent Himself, [br] You are a priest for ever”);
» The Lord swore and will not repent Himself :: Aaron's line was never sworn into office, only appointed; the oath of Psalm 110:4 makes Messiah's priesthood, uniquely, an irrevocable covenant grant rather than a revocable commission.
→ The Lord swore and will not repent Himself :: Psalm 110:4
22  by so much also has Yeshua become the surety of a better covenant.
23  And they indeed have been made priests many in number, [br] because that by death they are hindered from continuing:
24  but He, because He abides for ever, has His priesthood unchangeable.
25  Therefore also He is able to save to the uttermost them that draw near to God through Him, [br] seeing He ever lives to make intercession for them.
---
26  For such a high priest became us, holy, guileless, undefiled, [br] separated from sinners, and made higher than the heavens;
27  who needs not daily, like those high priests, to offer up sacrifices, first for His own sins, and then for the sins of the people: [br] for this He did once for all, when He offered up Himself.
28  For the Torah appoints men high priests, having infirmity; [br] but the word of the oath, which was after the Torah, appoints a Son, perfected for evermore.

## Hebrews 8

1  Now in the things which we are saying the chief point is this: We have such a high priest, [br] who sat down on the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens,
2  a minister of the sanctuary, and of the true tabernacle, which the Lord pitched, not man.
» the true tabernacle, which the Lord pitched, not man :: Second Temple Jewish tradition already spoke of a heavenly sanctuary of which the earthly one was a copy (cf. 1 Enoch 14; the Qumran *Songs of the Sabbath Sacrifice*); the author stands within that tradition rather than inventing it, then argues the heavenly original is where Messiah now ministers.
3  For every high priest is appointed to offer both gifts and sacrifices: [br] therefore it is necessary that this high priest also have somewhat to offer.
---
4  Now if He were on earth, He would not be a priest at all, [br] seeing there are those who offer the gifts according to the Torah;
5  who serve that which is a copy and shadow of the heavenly things, even as Moses is warned of God when he is about to make the tabernacle: [br] for, “See, says He, that you make all things according to the pattern that was showed you in the mount.”
→ that you make all things according to the pattern that was showed you in the mount :: Exodus 25:40
6  But now has He obtained a ministry the more excellent, [br] by so much as He is also the mediator of a better covenant, which has been enacted upon better promises.
» the mediator of a better covenant :: *mesitēs* — the term used of Moses at Sinai in Jewish tradition (made explicit at Galatians 3:19); the author claims for Messiah the very role Moses held at the first covenant's inauguration.
7  For if that first covenant had been faultless, [br] then would no place have been sought for a second.
---
8  For finding fault with them, He says, [br] “Behold, the days come, says the Lord, [br] That I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah;
» That I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah :: *brit chadashah* — the only occurrence of the phrase "new covenant" in the entire Hebrew Bible; the author quotes Jeremiah's own promise to Israel and Judah by name, and its addressees do not change even as it is now announced as inaugurated.
→ I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah :: Jeremiah 31:31
9  Not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers [br] In the day that I took them by the hand to lead them forth out of the land of Egypt; [br] For they continued not in my covenant, [br] And I regarded them not, says the Lord.
