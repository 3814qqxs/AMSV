# Titus

## Titus 1

1  Paul, a servant of God, and an apostle of Yeshua Messiah, [br] according to the faith of God’s elect, [br] and the knowledge of the truth which is according to godliness,
2  in hope of eternal life, [br] which God, who cannot lie, promised before times eternal;
→ who cannot lie :: Numbers 23:19
3  but in His own seasons manifested His word in the message, [br] with which I was entrusted according to the commandment of God our Saviour;
4  to Titus, my true child after a common faith: [br] Grace and peace from God the Father and Messiah Yeshua our Saviour.
>>>
5  For this cause left I you in Crete, [br] that you should set in order the things that were deficient, [br] and appoint elders in every city, as I gave you charge;
→ appoint elders in every city :: Acts 14:23
6  if any man is blameless, [br] the husband of one wife, [br] having faithful/obedient children, [br] who are not accused of dissolute living or unruly.
→ the husband of one wife :: 1 Timothy 3:2
» husband of one wife :: *mias gynaikos andra* ("a one-woman man") — the Greek states the qualification as a settled character trait, not a marital-history checklist; the ASV's English idiom is retained in the text.
→ dissolute living :: Luke 15:13
» dissolute living :: *asōtia* (ἀσωτίᾳ) — the same root as Luke 15:13's "wasteful/dissolute living" (*asōtōs*, ἀσώτως), the prodigal son's ruinous excess; an elder's household must not be marked by that same recklessness.
7  For the overseer must be blameless, as God’s steward; [br] not self-willed, [br] not quick-tempered, [br] not given to wine, [br] no striker, [br] not greedy of filthy lucre;
» overseer :: *episkopon* ("overseer, one who watches over") — traditionally rendered "bishop." Used interchangeably with "elder" (*presbyteros*, v5) for the same office, reflecting the Second-Temple synagogue eldership structure the earliest assemblies inherited.
8  but given to hospitality, [br] a lover of good, [br] sober-minded, just, holy, self-controlled;
9  holding to the faithful word which is according to the teaching, [br] that he may be able both to exhort in the sound doctrine, [br] and to refute the objectors.
>>>
10  For there are many unruly men, vain talkers and deceivers, [br] specially they of the circumcision,
» they of the circumcision :: refers to Judaizing teachers demanding Gentile believers keep Mosaic ritual law (cf. Acts 15; Galatians 2) — a live controversy in Crete's mixed Jewish-Gentile congregations.
11  whose mouths must be stopped; [br] men who overthrow whole houses, teaching things which they ought not, [br] for filthy lucre’s sake.
12  One of themselves, a prophet of their own, said, “Cretans are always liars, [br] evil beasts, [br] idle gluttons.”
» a prophet of their own :: the quotation is from Epimenides of Knossos, a 6th-century BC Cretan poet-philosopher venerated by some as a seer; Paul cites a pagan source the same way he does at Athens (Acts 17:28), meeting his audience on their own ground.
13  This testimony is true. [br] For which cause reprove them sharply, [br] that they may be sound in the faith,
14  not giving heed to Jewish fables, [br] and commandments of men who turn away from the truth.
→ Jewish fables :: 1 Timothy 1:4
» Jewish fables :: *Ioudaikois mythois* ("Jewish myths/legends") — likely the same speculative genealogies and legalistic embellishments addressed in 1 Timothy 1:4; not Torah itself, but rabbinic-style elaboration mistaken for authoritative tradition.
15  To the pure all things are pure: [br] but to them that are defiled and unbelieving nothing is pure; [br] but both their mind and their conscience are defiled.
» To the pure all things are pure :: sets moral/inward purity (*katharos*) against Levitical ritual-purity categories (clean/unclean foods, etc.); echoes Yeshua's own teaching that defilement is a matter of the heart, not of external contact (cf. Mark 7:15).
16  They profess that they know God; [br] but by their works they deny Him, [br] being abominable, and disobedient, and to every good work disqualified.

## Titus 2

1  But speak you the things which befit the sound doctrine:
2  that aged men be temperate, grave, sober-minded, sound in faith, in love, in patience:
3  that aged women likewise be reverent in demeanor, [br] not slanderers nor enslaved to much wine, [br] teachers of that which is good;
» slanderers :: *diabolous* (διαβόλους) — the same root rendered "devil" elsewhere (*ho diabolos*, "the slanderer/accuser"); one who slanders plays the accuser's role in miniature.
4  that they may train the young women to love their husbands, to love their children,
5  to be sober-minded, chaste, workers at home, kind, being in subjection to their own husbands, that the word of God be not blasphemed:
---
6  the younger men likewise exhort to be sober-minded:
7  in all things showing yourself an example of good works; in your doctrine showing uncorruptness, gravity,
8  sound speech, that cannot be condemned; that he that is of the contrary part may be ashamed, having no evil thing to say of us.
---
9  Exhort servants to be in subjection to their own masters, and to be well-pleasing to them in all things; [br] not talking back;
→ servants to be in subjection to their own masters :: Ephesians 6:5
10  not pilfering, but showing all good fidelity; that they may adorn the doctrine of God our Saviour in all things. [br]
11  For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation to all men,
» all men :: *anthrōpois* (ἀνθρώποις) — generic "humanity/people," not gender-specific (contrast *anēr*, "male"); the offer of grace is universal in scope.
12  instructing us, to the intent that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly and righteously and godly in this present world;
13  looking for the blessed hope and appearing of the glory of the great God and our Saviour Yeshua Messiah;
» the great God and our Saviour Yeshua Messiah :: *tou megalou Theou kai sōtēros hēmōn Iēsou Christou* — one definite article governs both "God" and "Savior" (a Granville Sharp construction), grammatically identifying Yeshua as *ho megas Theos*, "the great God" — one of the NT's clearest single-verse statements of His deity.
14  who gave Himself for us, that He might redeem us from all iniquity, and purify to Himself a people for His own possession, zealous of good works.
→ a people for His own possession :: Exodus 19:5
» a people for His own possession :: *laon periousion* (λαὸν περιούσιον) — echoes the LXX's *am segullah* (עַם סְגֻלָּה, "treasured possession") applied to Israel at Sinai (Exodus 19:5); the redeemed community inherits covenant language first spoken over Israel.
>>>
15  These things speak and exhort and reprove with all authority. [br] Let no man despise you.

## Titus 3

1  Put them in mind to be in subjection to rulers, to authorities, to be obedient, to be ready to every good work,
→ subjection to rulers, to authorities :: Romans 13:1
2  to speak evil of no man, not to be contentious, to be gentle, showing all meekness toward all men. [br]
3  For we also once were foolish, [br] disobedient, [br] deceived, [br] serving various lusts and pleasures, [br] living in malice and envy, [br] hateful, [br] hating one another. [br]
4  But when the kindness of God our Saviour, and His love toward man, appeared,
5  not by works done in righteousness, which we did ourselves, [br] but according to His mercy He saved us, [br] through the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Spirit,
» washing of regeneration :: *palingenesias* (παλιγγενεσίας) — used elsewhere in the NT only at Matthew 19:28, of the messianic renewal of the whole world; individual new birth here shares in that same cosmic renewal.
6  which He poured out upon us richly, [br] through Yeshua Messiah our Saviour;
→ poured out :: Joel 2:28
» poured out :: echoes Joel's promised outpouring of the Spirit (LXX *ekcheō*), fulfilled at Pentecost (Acts 2:17) and now applied to Titus's Cretan readers.
7  that, being justified by His grace, [br] we might be made heirs according to the hope of eternal life.
>>>
8  Faithful is the saying, [br] and concerning these things I desire that you affirm confidently, [br] to the end that they who have believed God may be careful to maintain good works. [br] These things are good and profitable to men: [br]
» Faithful is the saying :: *pistos ho logos* — a recurring formula unique to the Pastoral Epistles (1 Timothy 1:15; 3:1; 4:9; 2 Timothy 2:11), marking a fixed, creed-like statement of the community's core teaching.
9  but shun foolish questionings, [br] and genealogies, [br] and strifes, [br] and fightings about the Torah; [br] for they are unprofitable and vain.
» the Torah :: *nomikas* ("legal, Torah-related") — per the false teachers' Jewish-fables background (cf. Titus 1:14), these are rabbinic-style disputes over Torah interpretation, not Roman civil law.
10  A factious man after a first and second admonition refuse;
11  knowing that such a one is warped, [br] and sins, being self-condemned.
>>>
12  When I shall send Artemas to you, or Tychicus, [br] give diligence to come to me to Nicopolis: [br] for there I have determined to winter.
13  Set forward Zenas the lawyer and Apollos on their journey diligently, [br] that nothing be lacking to them.
14  And let our people also learn to maintain good works for necessary uses, [br] that they be not unfruitful.
>>>
15  All that are with me greet you. [br] Greet them that love us in faith. [br] Grace be with you all.
